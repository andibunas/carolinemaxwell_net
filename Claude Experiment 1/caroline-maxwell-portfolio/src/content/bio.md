Caroline Maxwell (b. 1986) is a painter working primarily in oil, gouache, and graphite. Her work returns again and again to domestic interiors — kitchens, studios, the corners of rooms where light collects and changes — treating each one as a slow, ongoing study rather than a single subject to be resolved.

She studied painting at the Rhode Island School of Design and later completed an MFA at Yale, where she began the working method she still uses: drawing a space from memory many times before painting it once. Her paintings are usually made over long stretches — months, sometimes a full year for a single small canvas — and she has become increasingly interested in slowness itself as part of the work's subject matter, not just its process.

Maxwell's paintings have been shown in group and solo exhibitions across the Northeast, and her writing on process and studio practice appears alongside her painting in the Writings section of this site. She lives and works in the Hudson Valley, in a converted barn studio with unreliable heat and excellent north light.

When she is not painting, she is usually drawing the same room again.
